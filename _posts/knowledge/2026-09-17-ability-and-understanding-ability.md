## What is an ability?

An **ability** is a relatively reliable capacity to perform a certain class of tasks under relevant conditions.

It is not merely possessing information once. Evidence of an ability is repeatable performance:

- a person can do the task again;
- can do it with reasonable independence;
- can handle some variation in the task;
- knows enough to notice or correct important errors.

Examples: remembering a sequence, sustaining attention, calculating, reading a graph, explaining an argument, or planning a task. Each is a different ability; no single example establishes an overall ranking of intelligence.

## What is understanding ability?

**Understanding ability** is the capacity to build and use a meaningful model of a topic rather than only repeat information about it.

For a particular concept, evidence of understanding includes the ability to:

1. **Explain** the relationship or mechanism: why does this happen?
2. **Predict** a changed case: if this condition changes, what should happen?
3. **Apply / transfer** the idea to a new but relevant case.
4. **Diagnose and revise**: identify a false assumption or repair the model when evidence conflicts with it.

## Relation to memory and attention

- **Attention** and **working memory** are cognitive supports: they help a learner hold and manipulate information while reasoning.
- **Memory** supplies facts, vocabulary, examples, and prior models.
- **Understanding** organises and uses these resources to reason about a topic.

Therefore:

> Strong memory or concentration can help build understanding, but does not by itself prove understanding. Conversely, an explanation plus transfer is stronger evidence of topic-specific understanding, but does not measure a person's entire cognitive ability.

## Concrete example

A learner can recite `F = ma`:

- This demonstrates **recall**.

The learner can state that, with net force fixed, doubling mass halves acceleration; can predict a new case; and can notice that friction changes the relevant force:

- This demonstrates stronger **understanding of the relation**.

## Practical test

For any topic, ask four questions:

1. Can I explain it without only repeating the definition?
2. Can I predict a changed condition?
3. Can I use it in a new example?
4. Can I find what assumption failed if my prediction is wrong?

If the answer is consistently yes, there is meaningful evidence of understanding ability for that topic.
