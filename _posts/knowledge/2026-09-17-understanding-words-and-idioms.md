## Core distinction

Knowing a translation or dictionary gloss is only **recognition**. Understanding a word or idiom means being able to recover its intended meaning in context and use that meaning appropriately.

## Understanding a word

For a word, evidence can include:

1. **Form:** recognise pronunciation/spelling and basic grammatical role.
2. **Contextual sense:** select the intended meaning when the word has more than one sense.
3. **Relations:** distinguish it from close alternatives, common collocations, and typical grammar.
4. **Use:** make or choose a natural sentence that fits the context and register.
5. **Repair:** notice and correct an inappropriate use.

A learner does not need every item equally for every goal. Reading requires reliable contextual recognition; speaking/writing requires productive and pragmatic use as well.

## Understanding an idiom

An **idiom** is an expression whose conventional meaning cannot reliably be obtained by translating each component word literally. Understanding an idiom includes:

1. recognise it as a fixed or semi-fixed expression;
2. infer or retrieve its intended non-literal meaning in context;
3. paraphrase the meaning plainly;
4. recognise the tone/register and when it is appropriate;
5. use it or respond to it naturally without literal misinterpretation.

## Examples

### Word: `run`

- `I run every morning.` → move quickly on foot.
- `She runs a shop.` → manages/operates it.

Understanding `run` requires choosing the intended sense from context, not recalling only one translation.

### Idiom: `spill the beans`

- `He spilled the beans about the surprise party.`
- Meaning: he revealed a secret.

Understanding it means recognising that it is not about dropping literal beans, paraphrasing it correctly, and knowing it is an informal expression commonly used for disclosing a secret.

## Four-level check

| Level | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Recognition | “I have seen this form and recall a gloss.” |
| Receptive understanding | “I can identify the intended meaning in this sentence.” |
| Productive understanding | “I can use it in a suitable original sentence.” |
| Flexible/pragmatic understanding | “I can distinguish close alternatives, register, and repair an incorrect use.” |

## Practical test

For a word or idiom, test:

1. What does it mean **here**?
2. What clue in the context tells me that?
3. Can I paraphrase it without using the same expression?
4. Can I use it in a new natural sentence—or explain why it would be unsuitable there?
5. Can I distinguish it from a close word or expression?
