## Core distinction

**Understanding** is the usable grasp of a topic's meaning, relations, mechanisms, and conditions. It is demonstrated when a learner can explain why something happens, predict consequences of a changed condition, and reason beyond a memorised wording.

**Mastery** is reliable, independent, and sufficiently flexible performance in a defined class of tasks. It includes understanding when the task requires conceptual judgment, but also requires repeated execution, error detection, and adaptation to the intended context.

> Understanding asks: **“Do I have a usable model of this?”**  
> Mastery asks: **“Can I perform reliably at the level this situation requires?”**

## Their relationship

1. Understanding commonly supports mastery because a model helps the learner choose methods, detect errors, and transfer to changed cases.
2. Practice toward mastery tests and improves understanding: errors reveal missing conditions, incorrect assumptions, or weak mental representations.
3. Neither simple recall nor a single correct answer proves mastery. A correct answer may come from guessing, imitation, or a memorised pattern.
4. Some skills also require procedural fluency. A learner may understand a principle but not yet perform it quickly, consistently, or under realistic constraints.

## Practical hierarchy of mastery

| Level | What the learner can do | What it does **not** yet establish |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Exposure / recognition | Recognise a word, rule, method, or example. | Independent explanation or use. |
| 2. Understanding / explanation | Explain the main idea, relationship, or reason in their own words. | Reliable execution in tasks. |
| 3. Guided application | Use the idea in a familiar example with prompts or a model. | Independent choice of method. |
| 4. Independent proficiency | Complete typical tasks accurately and repeatedly without prompts. | Transfer to substantially changed cases. |
| 5. Transfer / flexible mastery | Adapt the idea to unfamiliar, variable, or complex cases; choose between methods. | Broad expertise or innovation. |
| 6. Creation / teaching | Diagnose errors, compare approaches, improve a method, and teach the idea clearly. | Universal mastery outside the defined domain. |

## How to assess a claimed level

Use observable evidence rather than confidence:

- **Accuracy:** Is the result correct?
- **Stability:** Is performance reliable across more than one attempt?
- **Independence:** Does the learner need prompts, notes, or a worked example?
- **Transfer:** Can the learner handle a changed or unfamiliar case?
- **Reflection:** Can the learner locate and repair a relevant error?

## Example: vocabulary

For the word *maintain*:

- Recognition: recognises the word.
- Understanding: states a relevant meaning such as “keep something at a certain level or condition.”
- Guided application: completes a prompted phrase such as *maintain a healthy lifestyle*.
- Independent proficiency: uses it correctly in ordinary speech or writing.
- Transfer: distinguishes it appropriately from related words such as *sustain* and *preserve* in new contexts.
- Teaching: explains collocations, register, and a common misuse to another learner.

## Implication for this workspace

Do not record a concept as “mastered” merely because it was explained. Record the task conditions, the learner's unprompted performance, the remaining fragility, and the smallest next drill.
