- **Date added:** 2026-09-10
- **Purpose:** External evidence for the working claim that study time is not sufficient by itself; strategy, sleep/rest, planning, and feedback/reflection can alter learning outcomes.
- **Important boundary:** These studies support particular interventions in particular populations and tasks. They do **not** prove that every learner will improve, nor do they replace measuring the learner's own performance.
- **Source verification:** Bibliographic details, study design, and headline results were checked against PubMed records on 2026-09-10.

## 1. Retrieval practice and feedback

**Ludowicy, P., Paz-Alonso, P. M., Lachmann, T., & Czernochowski, D. (2023). _Performance feedback enhances test-potentiated encoding_. Frontiers in Behavioral Neuroscience, 17, 1100497. DOI: 10.3389/fnbeh.2023.1100497. PMID: 37153935.**

- **Design:** Two controlled experiments. Experiment 1 involved 40 participants; Experiment 2 independently replicated the pattern with 25 participants. Participants learned word pairs by restudying or retrieval/testing, with performance-contingent feedback in some conditions.
- **Result:** Final recall was better for tested than restudied material; performance feedback added a further later benefit.
- **What it supports:** “Effective method” can be operationalised as retrieval practice followed by feedback, rather than passive rereading alone.
- **Limit:** Laboratory word-pair retention, not a direct measure of the learner's course grade.

## 2. Sleep condition interacts with study schedule

**Huang, S., Deshpande, A., Yeo, S. C., Lo, J. C., Chee, M. W. L., & Gooley, J. J. (2016). _Sleep restriction impairs vocabulary learning when adolescents cram for exams: The Need for Sleep Study_. Sleep, 39(9), 1681–1690. DOI: 10.5665/sleep.6092. PMID: 27253768.**

- **Design:** Randomized parallel-group study of 56 adolescents aged 15–19. Participants had either 5 or 9 hours time in bed nightly for one week. Vocabulary was learned either in massed sessions or spaced across days, while total study time was held constant.
- **Result:** Sleep restriction impaired recall more strongly for massed study; spaced learning protected recall relative to massed learning under sleep restriction.
- **What it supports:** Equal study time can yield different retention depending on sleep opportunity and study schedule.
- **Limit:** Vocabulary recall in adolescents; it does not establish a universal grade effect.

## 3. Planning and reflection around academic resources

**Chen, P., Chavez, O., Ong, D. C., & Gunderson, B. (2017). _Strategic resource use for learning: A self-administered intervention that guides self-reflection on effective resource use enhances academic performance_. Psychological Science, 28(6), 774–785. DOI: 10.1177/0956797617696456. PMID: 28447894.**

- **Design:** Students in two cohorts of an introductory statistics class were randomly assigned before exams to a strategic-resource-use intervention or a control condition.
- **Intervention:** Students planned which resources they would use, why they would use them, and how.
- **Result:** Treated students reported greater self-reflection and outperformed controls by an average of roughly one-third of a letter grade in the course.
- **What it supports:** Planning and reflection can be studied as manipulable learning processes rather than vague advice.
- **Limit:** The intervention was a defined package; it does not show that any kind of planning or reflection always works.

## 4. Goal setting, elaboration, and reflection

**Morisano, D., Hirsh, J. B., Peterson, J. B., Pihl, R. O., & Shore, B. M. (2010). _Setting, elaborating, and reflecting on personal goals improves academic performance_. Journal of Applied Psychology, 95(2), 255–264. DOI: 10.1037/a0018478. PMID: 20230067.**

- **Design:** Randomized controlled intervention with 85 undergraduates experiencing academic difficulty; compared an online written goal-setting program with a control task over four months.
- **Result:** The goal-setting intervention group showed significantly improved academic performance relative to the control group.
- **What it supports:** Planning plus elaboration/reflection can affect an academic outcome under a defined intervention.
- **Limit:** This is not evidence that time alone causes improvement, and the study is specific to its participants and intervention.

## How to use these papers correctly

1. Treat them as **external evidence** that specific conditions can causally affect learning.
2. Do not cite them as proof that the exact whole sentence is universally true.
3. For personal verification, run a small within-subject comparison using comparable material, a fixed study-time budget, a predefined method, and delayed tests.
4. Record the outcome measures separately: immediate retrieval score, 7-day delayed retrieval score, error type, and time required.
