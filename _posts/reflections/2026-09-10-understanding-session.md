## What I practised

Distinguishing memorisation from understanding by reasoning about force, mass, supply/demand, and a claim about study time and grades.

## What I did first (before LLM help)

I answered a sequence of guided questions in my own words. The raw wording is preserved in [Thinking: What counts as understanding?](/thinking/understanding/).

## Evidence

- Correctly reasoned from `a = F/m` when force is fixed.
- Correctly identified inverse proportionality when mass doubles.
- Correctly rejected an overgeneralised claim about force and mass.
- Correctly identified that price and grades involve multiple relevant variables.

## What worked

Using a familiar relation with clearly fixed conditions made it possible to distinguish a remembered formula from a causal model.

## What was difficult

Moving from “there are many factors” to a precise conditional claim that says what must be held constant, what changes, and what result should follow.

## Earliest likely cause

The current reasoning notices omitted variables but has not yet consistently converted them into an explicit model with assumptions and a testable prediction.

## Lesson / correction rule

When a statement contains “always,” “certainly,” or a simple one-factor explanation, ask: **under what conditions, compared with what, and what other variable could reverse or weaken this conclusion?**

## Next smallest drill

The learner completed the positive claim and proposed experimental papers as external validation. The next improvement is precision: replace the stronger “才能” phrasing with a probabilistic claim and specify personal measurements. Design a comparison using matched material, a fixed time budget, immediate retrieval, seven-day retrieval, and an error log.
