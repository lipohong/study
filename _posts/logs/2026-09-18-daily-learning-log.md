## Video classification review

The learner requested a renewed analysis after supplying the original machine-generated KL02 subtitle file. The subtitle was read directly rather than reconstructed from an earlier summary. The revised analysis separates the video's useful teaching moves from its conceptual limits, empirical examples, and factual attributions.

The review found useful distinctions between an event, its measurement-based representation, data, contextual information, and reusable relations. It also found that the video treats different kinds of categories as one hierarchy, defines knowledge too narrowly, and omits the performance evidence needed to distinguish possession of a model from understanding and mastery.

Specific corrections were recorded: the attribution of DIKW to a 2014 David Williams article cannot establish its origin; the subtitle's simple migration-and-defeat account of *Homo sapiens* and Neanderthals is misleading; its inference from shared stories to a universal mobilisation recipe is unsupported; and its egg example changes from water above 95 C to water at or below 65 C for four minutes thirty seconds without explaining the change or supplying the thermal assumptions required by the model.

## Whole-map synthesis

The learner then requested a consolidated account of knowledge based on the project framework and the video. A paired English/Traditional-Chinese knowledge note now defines knowledge as relatively dependable, appropriately supported, context-specified, usable content or competence. It distinguishes world events, representations, measurement conventions, data, contextual information, knowledge resources, conceptual models, understanding, mastery, and decision-making. A Mermaid feedback-loop diagram uses a commute example to reduce the working-memory burden of holding these relations in prose.
