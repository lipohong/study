## Framework provided in response

### Relationship and difference

- **Understanding** concerns whether the learner can make sense of a concept: what it is, why it works, and which relations or mechanisms matter.
- **Mastery** concerns reliable performance: whether the learner can use the concept accurately, independently, and flexibly in relevant tasks.
- Understanding is usually a foundation for mastery, while attempts to apply a concept expose gaps and deepen understanding.
- Mere repetition or fluent explanation is insufficient evidence of either robust understanding or mastery.

### Working hierarchy of mastery

| Level | Label | Observable evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Exposure / recognition | Recognises a term or procedure when seen. |
| 2 | Understanding / explanation | Can explain the idea, relation, or procedure in their own words. |
| 3 | Guided application | Can complete a familiar task with an example, prompt, or model. |
| 4 | Independent proficiency | Can complete typical tasks accurately and reliably without prompts. |
| 5 | Transfer / flexible mastery | Can select and adapt the idea in unfamiliar, changed, or complex situations. |
| 6 | Creation / teaching | Can evaluate alternatives, diagnose errors, improve an approach, and teach it to another person. |

## Important qualification

The hierarchy is a practical diagnostic model, not a claim that every topic must reach level 6. The required level depends on the learner's real use case.
