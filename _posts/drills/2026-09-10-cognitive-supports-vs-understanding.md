- **Skill / subskill:** Distinguish cognitive supports from demonstrated understanding in a concrete case.
- **Starting level:** unknown
- **Timebox:** 5 minutes

## Performance condition

Answer in 3–5 sentences, without looking at the distilled note first:

> A can concentrate and remember well but only recites definitions. B has ordinary memory but explains causal relations and applies concepts to new examples. Who has stronger demonstrated understanding? What does A demonstrate? What cannot be concluded about either person's overall cognitive ability?

## Success criteria

- [ ] States that B shows stronger **demonstrated understanding of the topic** because B explains and transfers.
- [ ] States that A shows evidence of the specific supports named: concentration and memory.
- [ ] Does not conclude that A or B is globally “more intelligent” or has higher overall cognitive ability from this short description.
- [ ] Explains that cognitive supports help reasoning but are not the same as possessing a conceptual model.

## Common error to watch

- Treating one observed strength (such as memory) as proof of global intelligence or robust understanding.

## Next-step rule

- If all criteria are met: diagnose a real learning difficulty as primarily knowledge/model, cognitive load, or both.
- If only partly met: contrast one missing inference with a new example.
