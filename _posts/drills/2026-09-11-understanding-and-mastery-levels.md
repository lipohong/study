- **Date:** 2026-09-11
- **Related goal:** [Current Goal](/goals/current-goal/)
- **Skill / subskill:** Distinguish understanding from mastery, then identify the best-supported level of mastery from evidence.
- **Starting level:** unknown
- **Timebox:** 8 minutes

## Performance condition

Without opening the distilled note, classify each case below. For each one, write:

1. whether it demonstrates **understanding**, **mastery**, both, or neither;
2. the highest level supported by the evidence (1–6); and
3. one sentence explaining why the evidence does **not** justify a higher level.

### Cases

1. A learner can define *opportunity cost* accurately but cannot analyse a new purchase decision.
2. A learner solves ten standard percentage problems correctly without notes, but has not worked on word problems with unfamiliar wording.
3. A learner explains why a larger mass has less acceleration when net force is fixed, and correctly predicts what happens when the mass doubles.
4. A learner fixes an unfamiliar spreadsheet formula error, explains the cause, and teaches a colleague how to avoid it.

## Success criteria

- [ ] Does not treat recall alone as mastery.
- [ ] Distinguishes explanation/prediction from independent repeated performance.
- [ ] Uses “transfer” only when the case includes a genuinely changed or unfamiliar context.
- [ ] States an evidence-based reason for withholding a higher level.
- [ ] Uses the terms **understanding**, **independent proficiency**, and **transfer** consistently.

## Common error to watch

- Assigning the highest possible level because a learner performed one impressive action, without checking whether the performance was repeated, independent, or transferable.

## Next-step rule

- If all criteria are met: apply the same hierarchy to one real skill the learner is currently studying.
- If partly met: compare one pair of cases that differ only in whether the learner has a prompt or can transfer to a changed condition.
