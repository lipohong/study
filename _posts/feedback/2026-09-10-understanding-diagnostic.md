## Evidence-based feedback

### What the learner demonstrated

1. **Conditional quantitative reasoning — promising.** The learner rearranged `F = ma` into `a = F/m` and reasoned correctly under the stated condition that force is fixed.
2. **Proportional reasoning — promising.** The learner correctly concluded that doubling mass halves acceleration when net force is unchanged.
3. **Concept separation — promising.** The learner separated mass from received force and recognised that “harder to push” refers to a lower acceleration for the same net force, not automatically to less force.
4. **Multi-variable causal reasoning — promising.** The learner recognised that both supply and demand matter for price, and that study time is not the only determinant of grades.
5. **Understanding standard — promising.** The learner rejected both unexplained correct prediction and explanation that fails under changed conditions as complete understanding.

## Earliest limitation observed

The learner can identify that other factors exist, but has not yet shown the next step: **specify which conditions matter, how they affect the conclusion, and what prediction follows.**

For example, “time is only one factor” is correct, but it does not yet state the relationship among study time, study quality, prior knowledge, rest, and the assessment target.

## Minimal next correction

For every broad causal statement, use this short check:

1. What outcome am I explaining?
2. What variable is claimed to cause it?
3. What other conditions must be held roughly fixed, or explicitly included?
4. What changed-case prediction would show the claim is too strong?

## Drill attempt 01 — 2026-09-10

**Learner response:**

> 用錯學習方法，不顧身體情況，不做規劃，不做復盤反思，這樣下來不管時間增加多少成績不見到改善
>
> 當學習方法有效、身體狀況足以專注、並有規劃和復盤時，學習成績才能有所提升。
>
> 用論文的實驗報告來驗證。

### What this demonstrates

- Names multiple relevant conditions beyond time: learning method, physical condition, planning, and reflection/feedback.
- Supplies a coherent boundary case: extra study time need not improve grades when those conditions are poor.
- Correctly rejects the original unconditional claim.

### What the completed response demonstrates

- It now states a **positive conditional claim**: effective learning method, enough capacity to concentrate, planning, and review/reflection are named as relevant conditions.
- It identifies an outcome: academic performance can improve.
- It distinguishes **external evidence** (experimental papers) from unsupported opinion.

### Precision correction

The phrase **「才能有所提升」** is still stronger than the evidence warrants: it makes all listed conditions sound strictly necessary and almost sufficient. A more scientific form is:

> 當學習者採用有效方法、具備足以專注的身體狀況、並配合規劃與復盤時；在其他重要條件大致相近下，增加**有效的**學習投入通常較可能提升之後的測驗表現與長期保留。

This is better because it names the conditions, avoids certainty, and specifies measurable outcomes.

### Evidence correction

“Use experimental reports from papers” is an appropriate **external validation strategy**, not itself the outcome measure. The relevant reports have been recorded in [Experimental evidence relevant to study conditions](/sources/study-conditions-experimental-evidence/). They test components separately: retrieval practice/feedback, sleep plus spacing, and planning/reflection interventions.

For the learner's own verification, measure: (1) an immediate retrieval/practice-test score, (2) a delayed score after seven days, (3) error types, and (4) time spent. Compare a defined method with the learner's usual method on matched material.

## Recommended next evidence

Design one small personal comparison with comparable material and a delayed test. Do not treat a single course-grade change as proof because topic difficulty and assessment conditions also vary.
